# School Management System
## Problem Statement

**Project:** School Management System (SMS)  
**Organization:** Medium-Sized Private School  
**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** October 2026

---

## 1. Introduction

Managing academic, administrative, and financial operations efficiently is essential for the successful operation of educational institutions.

The school serves approximately 600 students across nine academic grade levels and 18 class sections, supported by 35 teachers and 10 administrative staff members.

The increasing volume of student records, academic information, and financial transactions creates a need for an organized and reliable information management system.

## 2. Current Situation

The school's administrative processes rely on manual procedures, paper-based records, and separate electronic files.

Student registration, teacher information, class assignments, academic grades, and tuition payments are managed through disconnected recordkeeping methods.

This approach requires additional administrative effort, complicates information retrieval, and limits coordination between departments.

## 3. Identified Problems

The major problems affecting school operations include:

| ID | Problem | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| P-01 | Manual student record management | Time-consuming registration, updates, and information retrieval. |
| P-02 | Fragmented institutional data | Duplicate records, inconsistent information, and limited data integration. |
| P-03 | Manual grade processing | Increased risk of calculation errors and delayed academic results. |
| P-04 | Inefficient fee management | Difficulties tracking payments, outstanding balances, and financial records. |
| P-05 | Limited access control | Increased risk of unauthorized access to sensitive information. |
| P-06 | Manual report preparation | Delayed reporting and additional administrative workload. |
| P-07 | Uncoordinated class and teacher information | Difficulties maintaining accurate class assignments and related records. |

## 4. Root Causes

The identified problems primarily result from:

1. The absence of a centralized school information system.
2. Heavy reliance on manual administrative procedures.
3. Limited integration between academic, administrative, and financial records.
4. Insufficient automation of data validation and calculations.
5. Inadequate mechanisms for managing user permissions and information access.

## 5. Consequences

If these problems remain unresolved, the school may continue to experience:

- Increased administrative workload and operational delays.
- Higher risks of inaccurate or inconsistent records.
- Difficulties monitoring academic and financial information.
- Reduced efficiency in information sharing and reporting.
- Greater exposure to data security and integrity risks.

These challenges can negatively affect operational efficiency, service quality, and management decision-making.

## 6. Proposed Direction

To address these problems, the school requires an integrated School Management System that centralizes institutional information and automates essential operations.

The proposed system will incorporate seven core modules:

1. Student Management
2. Teacher Management
3. Class Management
4. Grade Management
5. Fee Management
6. User Management
7. Reports Management

The system will adopt a hybrid architecture combining web-based access and selected desktop functionality, supported by centralized application services and a database.

This approach will improve information consistency, simplify administrative workflows, strengthen access control, and support timely reporting.

## 7. Problem Statement Summary

The fundamental problem is the lack of an integrated information system capable of efficiently managing the school's academic, administrative, and financial operations.

Existing manual and disconnected processes increase administrative workload, introduce risks of data inconsistency, and limit timely access to reliable information.

Therefore, a centralized School Management System is needed to streamline operations, improve data accuracy and security, and support effective institutional management.
